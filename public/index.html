Not a festival.

A burn is a gathering built by the people in it. There is nothing to watch, only things to join.

  • No lineup.Nobody is booked to entertain you.
  • Gifts, not sales.No vendors, no bartering, no sponsors. You share what you bring.
  • Then it's gone.We pack everything out and leave the beach as it was.

SaWadee Burn grew out of two small one-day burns in the jungle of Koh Phangan. This is the first full-length one.

The basics

Is it a festival? Is it Burning Man?

Neither. The people who come make all of it: there is no lineup, no main stage and no VIP area. We are inspired by the 10 Principles of Burning Man, but this is not an Official Burning Man event and is not affiliated with Burning Man Project.

I have never been to a burn. Can I come?

Yes. First-timers and seasoned burners are equally welcome. Come self-reliant, with what you need for six days.

Where, when and how big?

2-7 March 2027 in Khanom, on Thailand's Gulf coast. The exact site address goes out with your ticket. We plan for 1,000 to 1,500 people. It is our first full-length burn, so that is a plan rather than a promise.

How do I get a ticket?

Only on Ticketmelon, and only in advance: see the ticket rules.

Can I transfer my ticket, or get a refund?

All sales are final. There are no refunds unless the event is cancelled or postponed. If we don't sell enough tickets to make the event happen, all ticket payments will be refunded, minus Ticketmelon's processing fee. The rules for transfers and name changes are not published yet. If you need an answer before you buy, email ticketing@sawadeeburn.com.

How do I get there?

By ferry from Koh Samui or Koh Phangan, or by air to Nakhon Si Thammarat or Surat Thani and then by road. Pick your route on the map.

Camping and packing

Where do I sleep?

In your tent. Everyone camps: in a theme camp, or in free camping, which means camping outside a camp, not free entry. Bring shade and earplugs. Campervans and sleeping in cars are not settled yet, so don't book one.

What do I bring?

Enough for six days without shops: shelter and shade, sleeping gear, food, water, a reusable cup and plate, sun and rain cover, insect repellent, your own medication, a small first-aid kit, a torch with spare batteries, a battery pack, rubbish bags and sturdy shoes. The full list comes in the Survival Guide.

Is there food, water or power?

Plan as if there is none. Food is not for sale, and a gifted meal is a gift, not catering. Bring your own water: four litres a day each is a sensible baseline, and water on site is not confirmed yet. Power goes to approved art and camps only, so you cannot charge a phone.

What is there on site?

Toilets and showers, and medical and welfare cover. No shops and no public Wi-Fi. Don't count on phone signal: download your ticket and directions before you leave.

On site

Can I buy or sell anything?

No. No vendors, no paid services, no brand promotion and no bartering either. If an essential such as ice has to be sold, we will say so well ahead.

What about fire, cooking and swimming?

All three are still being settled with the venue. Assume nothing burns without written approval. Assume there are no lifeguards: nobody swims alone.

Can I take photos?

Ask first, every time, and ask again before you post. Coming to a burn is not consent to appear on the internet. Never photograph children without their parent. Professional shoots and drones need approval in advance.

What is not allowed?

Pets, personal generators, weapons, fireworks, sky lanterns, glitter, loose feathers, and drones without approval. Leaving anything behind: your rubbish goes home with you, all of it. Thai law applies completely, and so do the venue's rules. The one full list comes later.

People and care

Can I bring children or a pet?

Children, yes: a child must be registered, and a parent or legal guardian is responsible the whole time. There is no childcare. Write to ticketing@sawadeeburn.com for a child's ticket. Pets, no: it is too hot, too loud and too far from a vet. For a legally recognised assistance animal, contact us before you book.

What if I need help?

For a medical problem or immediate danger, get the emergency team. If you feel unsafe, go to the Safer Zone, a Nomad, the Welfare team or anyone on the organising team. Bring travel insurance that covers Thailand: ambulances and hospitals here are not free.

Is the site accessible?

We do not know yet. It is a coastal site with sand and uneven ground. Tell us what you need: ticketing@sawadeeburn.com.

How we build

We are inspired by and plan to operate in keeping with the 10 Principles of Burning Man*. Larry Harvey wrote them. Here they are in our own words.

  1. Radical Inclusion

    Anyone may take part. We welcome the stranger. There are no conditions for belonging.

  2. Gifting

    A gift is given freely. Nothing is expected back, not even a trade.

  3. Decommodification

    No sponsors, no buying and selling, no advertising. The gift culture stays clear of commerce.

  4. Radical Self-Reliance

    Trust what you carry in you, and take care of your own needs.

  5. Radical Self-Expression

    You decide what your gift is. Offer it with respect for the people who receive it.

  6. Communal Effort

    We build, support and protect our shared spaces and art together.

  7. Civic Responsibility

    We look after each other and follow local law: here, the laws and customs of Thailand.

  8. Leaving No Trace

    We clean up after ourselves, take out what we brought, and leave the land better if we can.

  9. Participation

    Change comes from taking part fully. Doing rather than watching.

  10. Immediacy

    Be here now. Drop what stands between you and the people and place around you.

* The original text is on the Burning Man website. The principles belong to Burning Man Project.

We are free to express ourselves. When what we do touches another person's experience, consent comes first.

Consent is freely given, informed, specific, clear, and can be taken back at any moment. Ask before you touch, join in, step into someone's space, hand over anything, or point a camera. Silence is not a yes. If it's not a clear yes, don't assume. Ask.
